## 1. What the Track Market is

The Track Market lets people who play HoverNet publish tracks they have made so that other players can download and race them. It is run by [OWNER: the name of the person or organisation that runs HoverNet].

## 2. Who may publish

- You must have a signed-in HoverNet / Outiva account and be old enough to agree to these terms where you live. [OWNER/LAWYER: minimum age; the account privacy policy has the details.]
- During the private beta only invited authors can publish.

## 3. The author declaration (the text the author accepts when they publish)

> I made this track, or I have the right to publish it. It does not copy someone else's work without permission. I choose the licence shown below and I understand that anyone may download, use, copy and share the track under that licence, including HoverNet and the people who run HoverNet servers. The name, description and anything else I enter are mine to publish and are not unlawful, abusive or misleading, and contain no private information about anyone. I understand the track may be reviewed, unlisted or removed as described in the terms.

## 4. Licence

You choose one of these for each track, and it is shown with the track:

| Licence | In short |
| --- | --- |
| CC0 1.0 (`CC0-1.0`) | No conditions: anyone may do anything with the track |
| CC BY 4.0 (`CC-BY-4.0`) | Anyone may use and share it if they credit you (the market shows your name where the track is chosen and after a race) |
| CC BY-SA 4.0 (`CC-BY-SA-4.0`) | As CC BY, and changed versions must be shared under the same licence |

Licences that forbid commercial use or changes are not accepted, because servers and mirrors need to be able to distribute every track and the project needs to be able to convert tracks to future formats. You keep your rights; the licence does not transfer ownership. [LAWYER: check that these three licences and the wording above are enough for HoverNet to host, mirror and convert tracks, and what is needed beyond the licence, for example a limited licence for hosting if an author later withdraws.]

## 5. What may not be published

Tracks, names and descriptions must not: infringe anyone's rights; be unlawful, hateful, harassing or sexually explicit; contain personal information; contain anything that tries to harm a player's computer or the service; or imitate the project or another person. Tracks are data only: the market refuses any track that is not made of the game's own textures, backgrounds and elements. [LAWYER: local content rules for the countries you serve.]

## 6. Review, reports and removal

- Your first track is reviewed by a moderator before it is listed. After that, tracks list automatically when the automatic checks pass.
- Anyone can report a track. A track under report for possibly unlawful content is hidden while it is looked at.
- We may unlist or remove a track, and suspend a publisher, that breaks these terms. We will say why when we can.
- You can unlist your own track at any time. Players who already downloaded it keep the copy they have under its licence; the track is no longer offered, and official servers stop hosting it.

## 7. Rights complaints (takedown)

If you think a track in the Track Market infringes your rights, write to [OWNER: contact address] with: who you are; which track (its name and author, or its hash shown in the market); what right you own and why the track infringes it; and a statement that the information is correct. We act on a complete request within 14 days, and sooner for content that is plainly unlawful. The removed track's hash stays on a list so it is not offered again, and we keep the request but not the track. ## 8. Your data

The market keeps your account id, the time, the declaration version and the track's hash with each submission, for moderation and complaints. Your published name is the display name you choose; your email address is never shown. See the account privacy policy ([account-privacy-policy-draft.md](account-privacy-policy-draft.md)). On request we remove the link between your account and your tracks, but keep the record that a track was published under its licence.
